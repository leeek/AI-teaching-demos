Stage 1: Define your target population

Who does this brand actually serve? Describe those people as distributions, not labels. Demographics alone will not unlock the next stage. You also need at least one category behavior, one psychographic, and one laddered higher-order need.

Your population

Where do the numbers come from? Your secondary research: annual reports, Mintel, census data, what the brand discloses about its customers. These are assumptions, and your appendix reports them, so be ready to defend each one. Use the preview below to check that the shape matches what you know.

Stage 2: Draw a subject

Attributes are drawn in your browser from your Stage 1 distributions, without replacement. ChatGPT is never asked to invent a person. The tool then composes a backstory prompt with an anchor line that keeps this subject from drifting toward the average.

Stage 3: Interview

Write your questions once. They are reused for every subject, so answers can be compared and aggregated. For each question the tool composes a prompt with a required answer format. Paste it into that subject's ChatGPT thread, then paste the answer back here.

Your instruments

Type {{brand}} in a question to insert the brand name from the top bar. Leave it out when you want unaided recall, for example "which hospitals come to mind first?"

Stage 4: Build the sample and watch the variance

Repeat the draw and interview loop. A comfortable sample is 20 or more. The monitor below flags when answers start collapsing toward one value, which is the mean regression problem from lecture. Its thresholds are heuristics, not a human benchmark.

Sample tray

Stage 5: Export for the audit

Findings go in the report body. Instruments and method go in the appendix. Everything exports as plain text and CSV. Persona bios are not exported, because the audit reports aggregated evidence, not characters.
