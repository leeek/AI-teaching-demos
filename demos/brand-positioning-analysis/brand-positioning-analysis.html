Step 1: Identity Brand-controlled evidence

Identity is what the company itself knows and says about the brand: the founder story, the annual report, the brand's history back to its birth. Gather that evidence here and write the positioning statement the brand intends to own. It travels forward and gets tested in Step 3. Do not judge yet whether consumers see any of it.

Watch for contamination. "People think it's too expensive" is image, not identity. If a phrase describes how consumers currently see the brand, it belongs in Step 2, not here.

Step 2: Image Consumer and marketplace evidence

Image lives in the mind of the consumer: reviews, Reddit threads, interviews, social chatter. It can echo the identity, and it can carry distorted or spurious associations the brand never intended. Both belong here. Same rule-based grouping as Step 1, and your themes carry straight into Step 3 on their own.

Step 3: Positioning Analysis

Works from exactly the theme groups you built in Steps 1 and 2, statement included. Keeps the transmitted / absent / unintended split and adds a direct test of the stated position.
